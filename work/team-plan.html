#TeamPlan

Team plan

Building Runway’s teams business

Role
Design lead
Timeline
3 weeks · September 2026
Scope
In-product experience and marketing page

Small business owners were our largest source of recurring non-Enterprise revenue, yet they were paying as individuals, with no plan built for teams. In nine days from soft launch, Team brought in ~$500K, roughly $300K of it net new, across ~600 seats.

I owned the plan’s in-product experience where I designed an invite flow that shows seat count and cost before anyone hits send, and upsell dialogs that sell the feature someone just tried to use. I also moved collaboration features behind the Team plan, kept admin settings like SSO as an Enterprise upsell, and built the marketing page on our CMS.

Getting there meant aligning Product, Prosumer and Enterprise on an open-ended brief. As a next step, domain routing and discoverable projects will help new members find their team’s workspace.

Invite experience

Know the cost before you hit send

Seat count and cost are visible before anyone sends an invite. Working with Product, we set the self-serve limit at nine seats based on a competitive analysis and a revenue calculation. Past nine, a team is worth more on Enterprise, so the dialog routes it to Sales.

Upsell dialogs

Sell the feature someone just tried to use

The contextual dialog sells the feature someone just reached for. The default dialog compares billing and seats.

Feature gating

What makes Team worth it

Commenting, Brand Kit, Projects, Shared skills and Custom voices moved to the Team plan. Settings such as Model Configuration, SSO and analytics stay with Enterprise, so Team workspaces see an upgrade path to it.

Marketing website

Making Team plan more discoverable

We first launched Team without a marketing page, then realized we needed one place that showed every Team feature, and more ways for people to find the plan. I built the page on our CMS, from the content to the visuals, and optimized it for search (SEO) so Team shows up beyond the product itself.

Alignment

Aligning three teams on one plan

An open-ended brief

The brief caused confusion and took several meetings to settle the plan’s core features. I ran alignment sessions that each ended with clear next steps for every stakeholder.

Pushback on gating commenting

The Prosumer team resisted moving commenting to the Team plan. We gated it after all, and next time I’d bring the Prosumer team in much earlier.

Cannibalization risk

Enterprise and Sales worried Team would pull customers away from Enterprise. I partnered with Product to track Enterprise downgrades and lost leads after launch.

What’s next?

Domain routing

Team workspaces averaged just two members, a sign that teammates were starting their own workspaces instead of joining one. With domain routing, new sign-ups from a company domain find their team’s existing workspace first.
